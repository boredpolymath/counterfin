FDCPA Consumer Phone Defense Reference

1-Minute deflection phrase for collector incoming calls
MANDATORY OPENING STATEMENT (READ THIS FIRST):
"Before we begin, please be advised that this call is being documented. State your full legal name, the corporate name of your agency, your physical corporate address, and the original creditor's account number."
FORMAL DISPUTE & VALIDATION DEMAND:
"Pursuant to the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1692g, I dispute this alleged debt in its entirety. I demand formal verification in writing, including the original itemized hospital accounting and proof of your authority to collect. You must cease further telephone contact."
CRITICAL RULES — WHAT NEVER TO SAY:
  • DO NOT admit or acknowledge owning the debt.
  • DO NOT make a "good faith" token payment (this resets the Statute of Limitations clock).
  • DO NOT provide banking details, debit cards, or Social Security numbers.

Medical Debt Dispute Guides & Statutory Rights

Federal protections under FDCPA, FCRA & No Surprises Act

1. The No Surprises Act (Public Law 116-260)

Protects patients from surprise balance billing for emergency services and non-emergency services provided by out-of-network clinicians at in-network facilities. Providers cannot bill you more than your in-network cost-sharing amount.

2. 30-Day Debt Validation Window (15 U.S.C. § 1692g)

Sending a written Debt Validation Letter (DVL) via USPS Certified Mail within 30 days of the initial notice freezes all collection efforts. Collectors are legally barred from contacting you or furnishing adverse reports to credit bureaus until verification is provided.

3. The Statute of Limitations Reset Trap

Making even a $5 or $10 payment restarts the statutory clock in many states, exposing you to refreshed lawsuit liability. Never make partial payments while an account is under dispute.
