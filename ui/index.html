FDCPA Consumer Debt Compendium & Statutory Rights

Federal protections under FDCPA, CFPB Regulation F, FCRA & No Surprises Act

1. 30-Day Mandatory Debt Validation Stay (15 U.S.C. § 1692g)

Sending a written Debt Validation Letter (DVL) via USPS Certified Mail within 30 days of the initial notice freezes all collection efforts across all covered debts (credit cards, medical, loans, utilities). Collectors are legally barred from contacting you or furnishing adverse reports to credit bureaus until verification is provided.

2. Credit Card Junk Debt Buyers & CFPB Regulation F (12 CFR Part 1006)

Debt buyers (such as Midland, Portfolio Recovery, LVNV) purchase defaulted credit card portfolios for pennies on the dollar, frequently lacking proper documentation. Under CFPB Regulation F § 1006.34, they must furnish itemized validation disclosures, an unbroken chain of title from the original card issuer, and full charge-off fee itemization.

3. The No Surprises Act for Medical Debts (Public Law 116-260)

Protects patients from surprise balance billing for emergency services and non-emergency services provided by out-of-network clinicians at in-network facilities. Providers cannot bill you more than your in-network cost-sharing amount.

4. Time-Barred Debt & The Statute of Limitations Trap (12 CFR § 1006.26)

Once a debt's state statute of limitations expires (e.g. 3 years in NY, 4 in CA/TX/FL), collectors are strictly forbidden by federal law from suing or threatening to sue. Making even a $5 token payment can restart the statutory lawsuit clock in many states. Never make partial payments while an account is under dispute.

5. Auto Loan Deficiencies & UCC Article 9 Protections

After vehicle repossession, creditors must give timely notice of sale (UCC § 9-611) and conduct a commercially reasonable sale (UCC § 9-610). If they fail to provide full accounting of gross auction proceeds, storage, and fees, they may forfeit all rights to collect any deficiency balance.
