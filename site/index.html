Common Inquiries

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about CounterFin’s architecture, legal defense capabilities, and offline privacy guarantees.

Is CounterFin truly 100% offline with zero cloud sync? ▼
Yes. CounterFin is engineered from the ground up to never initiate network socket connections. The Rust backend has zero telemetry dependencies. All encryption, document generation, and database operations occur exclusively on your local CPU and disk.
Does CounterFin provide formal legal advice? ▼
No. CounterFin is a self-help legal defense workstation and document automation tool designed to help consumers manage their records, enforce statutory deadlines, and assert codified rights. It does not replace a licensed attorney. If you have been served with a formal court summons or complaint, you should seek licensed legal counsel immediately.
How do statutory rule updates work without an internet connection? ▼
CounterFin uses an offline patch engine. When state laws or federal regulations update, new rule sets are distributed as air-gapped JSON files signed with Ed25519 digital keys. You can import these files offline via USB drive or manual file selection, and CounterFin cryptographically validates the signature before applying overrides.
Can I export my generated dispute letters to PDF? ▼
Yes. Every dispute letter can be generated, previewed with resolved placeholders, and exported directly as court-ready PDF documents, Markdown files, or plaintext formats. You can also track USPS Certified Mail numbers and delivery deadlines.
What is the license and is CounterFin free? ▼
CounterFin is completely free and open source software licensed under the permissive MIT License. You have complete freedom to inspect the source code, verify all cryptographic algorithms, and build the application from source.
LEGAL DISCLAIMER: CounterFin is an informational self-help legal defense workstation and record-keeping software suite. It does not provide legal representation or legal advice. Laws vary by jurisdiction and are subject to change. For specific courtroom representation or formal litigation advice, consult an attorney licensed in your state.
